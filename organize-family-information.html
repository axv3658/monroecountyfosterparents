Keeping family information organized

A simple way to keep everyday contacts, questions, and clothing needs in one place.

Some days, the hardest part of getting something done is finding the right number or remembering the question you meant to ask. A small, consistent place for everyday information can help.

Start with one place

Choose something you already use: a notebook, a folder, or a private digital note. It doesn’t need to be elaborate. Start with three headings: contacts, questions, and things to do.

Keep the people easy to find

Write down the names and contact details your agency has given you, including who to contact outside office hours. Keep the original contact card somewhere accessible. Add school and other everyday contacts as you need them.

The useful contacts section can help you find public numbers, but your agency’s instructions are the place to look for contacts specific to your family.

Give your questions a home

When a question comes up, jot it down with the person you want to ask. After a conversation, record any next step and who is following up. A short list is easier to use than a perfect system you rarely open.

Keep a running clothing list

Note current sizes, shoes that are getting tight, and any seasonal items you’re missing. Separate what you need soon from what can wait. When you’re ready, use that list to request clothing from the Foster Closet.

Make it easy to keep up

Take a few minutes when it works for you to cross out completed tasks and update contact details. Keep personal information in a private place, and avoid putting a child’s identifying information in shared calendars or public notes.

A simple starting point: one contact to save, one question to write down, and one task for this week.
